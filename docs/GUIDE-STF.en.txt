ST VIDÉO — PC-TO-ATARI ST VIDEO ENCODER

ST Vidéo prepares a video on your PC so you can watch it on your Atari,
with sound. Here's how to try your first clip.

MY STF TEST

I tested the player on my Atari STF with 1 MB of RAM and ACSI/SD storage.
The program also works with 512 KB.
At 12.5 fps it runs well and looks surprisingly smooth. At 25 fps it slows
down on my setup because the storage cannot keep up. I recommend starting
with 12.5 fps on an STF.

1. CHOOSE A VIDEO

Open a video on your PC: AVI, MP4, WEBM and other supported formats.
You can also try a URL. If it fails, use a local file instead.
Converting a local file works offline.
Choose an output folder and an Atari name containing 1 to 8 characters.

2. APPLY THE STF SETTINGS

Click “Recommended STF / ACSI settings”. This selects:
• 12.5 frames per second;
• Fixed pattern (stable) dithering;
• Optimised ST palette;
• Optimised STF audio (slower encoding);
• Light audio cleanup and quiet-source boost.

“Slower encoding” means the PC takes longer to prepare the sound.
The video still plays at normal speed on the Atari.
You can adjust each setting after applying the profile.

3. TRY A SHORT PREVIEW

Click “Try a 5-second preview”. The files go into an APERCU subfolder
inside your output folder. Check the picture, then try it on the Atari
before converting a long video.
If a name already exists, a number is added. Your old clips are kept.

For the picture, I recommend starting with the optimised palette and fixed
dithering. Try No dithering for cartoons, text and flat colours.
For gradients, compare Fixed pattern and Adaptive pattern.
Sharper can bring out detail. Very sharp can add obvious outlines or halos.
Video noise reduction softens grain, but may remove small details too.
Adjust it to suit your video.

4. COPY IT TO THE ATARI

When you're happy, click “Encode video”.
Copy <NAME>.STV and STFPLAY.PRG to your Atari drive, on the same partition.
Run STFPLAY.PRG in colour low resolution on a PAL machine.
Sound is already inside the STV. You don't need the WAV or LAV files for
this playback. Use the player supplied with the encoder.

If VIDEO.STV exists, it starts automatically. Otherwise, choose your clip
in the file selector.
• Right Shift: stop the video and choose another one.
• Escape: exit. Cancelling the file selector also exits.

5. WHY 12.5 FPS?

The PC prepares the pictures for the Atari, but the ST still has to read
them from its drive. That takes about 410 kB/s at 12.5 fps, or 819 kB/s
at 25 fps. Even storage advertised at 800–900 kB/s can struggle at 25 fps
once access delays and the driver are taken into account.

At 12.5 fps the video keeps its original duration. You see fewer pictures
per second, rather than slow-motion playback. Audio is prepared at
8,192 Hz, compared with 12,288 Hz at 25 fps. LAV export always stays at 25 fps.

If playback slows down, encode again at 12.5 fps and copy the new STV.
Changing a setting does not change a file you have already encoded.
The <NAME>-REPORT.json file records the settings you used.
If it still slows down, check your storage speed and driver.
Hatari's Windows-folder drive does not reproduce real ACSI performance.
The player has also been tested in Hatari at 8 MHz with 512 KB.

6. WHAT ABOUT THE STE?

Choose “Optimised STE audio” for its cleaner DMA sound.
A clip encoded for STF still uses YM sound on an STE. To get DMA sound,
encode it again using the STE setting.
STF mode improves YM2149 playback, but won't match STE audio quality.
Start with Light cleanup. Strong can remove too much treble.
Quiet-source boost raises low volume by up to roughly +12 dB.
Lightweight YM is experimental; I recommend the optimised STF mode.

The drive still has to keep up, even on an STE. You can use 12.5 fps
with STE audio if playback struggles at 25 fps.

7. FILE SIZES AND LONG VIDEOS

STV takes about 24.6 MB per minute at 12.5 fps, or 49.2 MB at 25 fps.
The full folder is bigger because it includes LAV exports, WAV audio,
previews and a report. Everything goes into your chosen output folder.

For a long film, use Start and Duration to make separate parts before
reaching the 2 GiB file limit. The player does not yet play the parts
automatically one after another.

Use the Language selector at the top to switch between French and English.
Your choice is remembered without changing your encoding settings.
